Why structure matters now

More people get an answer before they click.

Google still sends readers to websites. Many searches now open with an AI Overview, though, and a growing number of people ask ChatGPT, Perplexity or Gemini directly. These tools look for short, clear passages on pages they can understand and trust.

That raises the bar for content. A page has to answer the question plainly, show where its facts come from, and be organised so each section makes sense on its own.

The SEO basics haven't gone anywhere. Search intent, keywords, headings, meta data and internal links are part of every piece I write. GEO adds a layer on top of them.

What I build into every article

  • A direct answer near the topThe reader, or an AI tool, gets the point in the first few lines.
  • Headings that follow real questionsSections mirror what people type into Google and ask AI assistants.
  • Named, credible sourcesFigures link to the authority behind them, such as IRAS, ACRA or Singapore's Ministry of Health.
  • Tables for anything comparedCosts, deadlines and options are easier to scan, and easier for search systems to read.
  • FAQs drawn from search dataFollow-up questions from People Also Ask, answered in under 100 words.

How I work

Four steps, with your approval at the one that matters most

The outline is where most problems get caught, so you see it before I write a full draft.

  1. Brief

    You send the topic, audience, market, target keyword and any sources or style guide. I check search intent and what currently ranks, and flag gaps before writing anything.

  2. Outline

    I map the headings, quick answer, tables and FAQs, and list the sources I plan to use. You approve it before drafting starts.

  3. Draft

    I research and write to the approved outline, check each figure against its source, and deliver in Google Docs or Word with a meta title, meta description and source list.

  4. Revision

    Two rounds of revisions within the original brief, requested within 7 days of delivery. Changes to the brief itself are quoted separately.

FAQ

Questions clients usually ask

What types of content do you write?

Long-form SEO and GEO articles, content refreshes and consolidation, website and service page copy, FAQ sections, product pages and buying guides. I also write press releases and LinkedIn profiles for subject experts, and review short-form video scripts.

Do you work with SEO and GEO?

Yes, both. SEO covers what helps Google understand and rank a page: search intent, keywords, headings, meta data and internal links. GEO (generative engine optimisation) adds structure that makes key passages easy for AI tools to pick up, such as direct answers, question-led headings, tables and named sources. Every article I write uses both.

Do you work with marketing agencies?

Yes, and white-label work is welcome. My content work has been inside an agency, so briefs from link builders, client approval rounds, style guides and monthly quotas are familiar. I can follow your templates and deliver without my name on the piece.

Which industries do you cover?

My published work covers health and wellness, accounting and tax, corporate services, company registration, cleaning and pest control, and organic health products, mostly for readers in Singapore and Thailand. I'm happy to take on other B2B and service topics that need careful research.

Do you accept part-time or ongoing projects?

I write part-time, up to four articles a week across all clients. Monthly packages work best, and one-off projects are fine when there's room in the schedule.

How does the paid trial work?

Send the trial form with your topic and brief. I reply within 24 hours on weekdays to confirm scope and deadline, then send an invoice with payment instructions. Writing starts once payment is received, and the article arrives within 4 business days. Sending the form is a request, not a booking.

Is the trial fee credited towards a package?

Yes. If you start a monthly package after the trial, the $120 comes off your first invoice.

How long does delivery take?

A trial article takes 4 business days from payment. For packages we agree a schedule at the start, usually one or two articles a week. Larger consolidation projects can take longer; I confirm the timeline before starting.

Do you take on content refreshes and consolidation?

Yes. I review the existing posts, check every figure against current sources, decide what to keep, merge or cut, and write one updated article. Consolidation starts at $200 per merged article.

How do you handle health and compliance-sensitive content?

Carefully. I avoid absolute claims, use cautious wording such as "may" and "can help", cite health authorities and hospitals rather than blogs, and point readers to a doctor where it matters. I'm not a medical professional, so I work with your reviewer or subject expert, and clinical points should be checked before publishing.

Do you guarantee Google rankings or AI citations?

No. Search engines and AI tools decide what they show, so nobody can honestly guarantee a ranking or a citation. What I can promise is the work itself: clear answers, sound structure, credible sources and a close match to search intent, built on SEO and GEO principles.
